Global Industry Shifts

US Section 301 Tariffs on ASEAN — New Rates Effective

The United States introduced a new round of tariffs on seven ASEAN countries, replacing the temporary 10% levy that expired 24 July 2026. Cambodia, Indonesia and Malaysia face 10%; the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand and Vietnam face 12.5%. The measures apply alongside existing import duties, with some exemptions.

→ Downstream SEA impact: Vietnam and Thailand electronics exporters face higher landed costs into the US; Malaysia and Indonesia comparatively advantaged at 10%.
Seasia Stats · 24 July 2026

China+1 Strategy Deepens Across SEA Electronics Manufacturing

Southeast Asia electronics manufacturing services market estimated at USD 28.91 billion in 2026, growing to USD 46.59 billion by 2031 at 10.01% CAGR. Expanding FDI, government incentives, and the China+1 diversification push are elevating ASEAN as a preferred production base.

→ Downstream SEA impact: Vietnam, Malaysia, Philippines and Indonesia absorbing mid-to-low-end assembly, testing and packaging operations.
Mordor Intelligence · 2026

Chinese Appliance Brands Gain Share in SEA

Chinese brands are gaining on Japanese counterparts across Southeast Asia in TVs and air conditioners. In home laundry appliances, Chinese brand share rose to 21.2% from 18.1%; in Thailand, share climbed to 23.2% from 15.3%. Haier and Midea scaling up production in Thailand.

→ Downstream SEA impact: Price competition intensifying in AC and laundry categories; Japanese brands under margin pressure.
Nikkei Asia · 22 June 2026

SEA Smartphone Market Shifts to Profitability Over Volume

Regional smartphone shipments fell 9% YoY in 1Q26 while ASPs rose to a record US$349. Rising DRAM and NAND costs force a pivot toward margins. Samsung reclaimed top position with 19% share (4.3M units); OPPO second at 20% but down 17% YoY; Xiaomi third at 17%, down 12%.

→ Downstream SEA impact: Entry-level segment under most pressure; vendors prioritising margin over shipment share.

Policy Focus

🇮🇩 Indonesia — MOT Reg 19/2026: E-Commerce Overhaul

Minister of Trade Regulation No. 19 of 2026 replaces MOT Reg 31/2023. Expands regulatory coverage to eight business models including ride-hailing platforms and online travel agents. Introduces Indonesia's first AI governance rules in commercial context, mandates algorithmic changes for domestic product promotion, and makes platforms directly responsible for seller compliance. Five-tier sanctions framework backs enforcement.

Enacted 8 June 2026 · Replaces Reg 31/2023
Compliance Alert: Cross-border sellers face tightened conditions; platforms must actively oversee merchant pricing and product traceability.

Mutual Recognition Agreements exist with Finland, Germany, UK and South Korea. PolicyBREAKING

MOT Reg 19/2026: Indonesia's E-Commerce Overhaul with AI Governance

Indonesia's Minister of Trade issued Regulation No. 19 of 2026 on Electronic Commerce, replacing MOT Reg 31/2023. The regulation expands coverage to eight business models, tightens conditions for cross-border sellers, mandates algorithmic changes for domestic product promotion, and introduces Indonesia's first AI governance rules in a commercial context.

Platforms are now directly responsible for merchant compliance, pricing practices, consumer-facing information and product traceability. A five-tier sanctions framework backs enforcement. The regulation aims to support MSMEs and strengthen consumer protection.
