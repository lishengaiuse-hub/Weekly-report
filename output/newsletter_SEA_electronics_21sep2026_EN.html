Global Industry Shifts

Structural moves with direct Southeast Asia downstream impact

China Plus One Becomes Permanent Structural Shift

Over 73% of Fortune 500 industrial companies now operate at least two or three parallel manufacturing nodes across Southeast Asia. Analysts describe the current state as "China Plus 0.5" rather than a true replacement, since rising ASEAN exports track closely with rising ASEAN imports of raw materials.

→ Downstream SEA impact: Vietnam and Malaysia absorb the bulk of assembly migration; Singapore consolidates as regional logistics and HQ node.
2026 · AsiaCommerce

Chinese Consumer Brands Accelerate SEA Expansion via M&A and E-Commerce

Chinese consumer brands are pushing deeper into home appliance and cosmetics markets once dominated by Japanese, South Korean and European companies, buying rivals and leveraging online sales channels. A Euromonitor report notes ASEAN economies account for 95% of the region's $4 trillion GDP.

→ Downstream SEA impact: Pricing pressure on incumbent Japanese and Korean appliance brands across Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand and Vietnam.
2026 · KrASIA / Nikkei Asia

Japanese and Korean Firms Shift Production Focus Toward Southeast Asia

Japanese and Korean firms are reducing reliance on China and shifting production capacity or investment to countries including Southeast Asia. Japan's electronics industry has been undergoing drastic restructuring, with a marked shift away from consumer electronics toward industrial electronics.

→ Downstream SEA impact: Vietnam is the clearest case — a China Plus One production base that has become one of the region's most dynamic consumer markets.
2026 · Blackbox JP

Chinese Appliance Brands Gain Ground on Japanese Rivals in SEA

Chinese brands are gaining on their Japanese counterparts across Southeast Asia, with TVs and air conditioners driving gains as prices and features appeal to younger buyers. Policy Focus

Regulatory changes affecting market access and compliance

🇮🇩 Indonesia — Permendag 19/2026 Overhauls E-Commerce Regulation

On 8 June 2026, Indonesia's Minister of Trade enacted Regulation No. 19 of 2026 on Electronic Commerce, replacing Regulation No. 31 of 2023. The new regulation widens the categories of businesses subject to PMSE rules, tightens conditions for cross-border sellers, mandates algorithmic changes for domestic product promotion, introduces Indonesia's first AI governance rules in a commercial context, and makes platforms directly responsible for the compliance of the sellers they host.

Status: In force · Five-tier sanctions framework
⚠ Platforms (Tokopedia, Shopee, Lazada, Blibli) increasingly require proof of TKDN before listing electronics.
2026 · ABNR · Nusantara DFDL

🇮🇩 Indonesia — TKDN Local Content Enforcement Tightens on Devices

Operators (Telkomsel, Indosat, XL, Tri) block IMEIs of devices without complete certification, meaning customers cannot use Indonesian SIM cards. Non-compliance risks include network activation bans, inability to be sold legally, and tax/duty clawback risk if devices are found being sold without proper TKDN. Indonesia's Ministry of Industry has reformed its TKDN policy to make certification more transparent, accountable and efficient.

Status: Enforced · Certification reform ongoing
⚠ Customs and tax authorities can demand reclamation of preferential duties on non-compliant devices.
2026 · Kickrate · RRI

🇲🇾 Malaysia — MCMC New Communication Equipment Standards

Malaysia's MCMC has set new standards for communication equipment, effective from May 5, 2026, with a six-month grace period. All subsequent related equipment submitted for certification must comply with these two new standards. The new regulations will be fully enforced starting November 5, 2026.

Status: Grace period · Full enforcement 5 Nov 2026
⚠ Online shopping platforms must update listing policies to require proof of SIRIM certification from local sellers.

🇸🇬 Singapore — CSA/IMDA Raise Router Cybersecurity Requirements to CLS Level 2

The Cyber Security Agency of Singapore (CSA) will work with the Infocomm Media Development Authority (IMDA) to raise mandatory cybersecurity requirements for residential routers from Cybersecurity Labelling Scheme (CLS) Level 1 to Level 2 by 2027. The CLS, introduced in 2020, rates the cybersecurity levels of IoT devices. Certification for Wi-Fi routers is mandatory.

Status: Announced · Effective by 2027
⚠ Manufacturers and distributors of residential routers must prepare to meet heightened security benchmarks to maintain market access.

🇻🇳🇹🇭🇵🇭 Vietnam, Thailand, Philippines — Consumer Electronics Tariff Structure 2026

Under ITA commitments, ICs (HS 8542) and laptops (HS 8471) enter at 0% across the Philippines, Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia and Indonesia. VAT/GST rates are 12% (Philippines), 10% (Vietnam), 7% (Thailand) and 0% SST 10% (Malaysia).

Status: In force · ATIGA and RCEP preferences available

Singapore CSA Cyber Trust Mark Requirements Set Staged Deadlines

New CSA Cyber Trust Mark requirements apply on a staged basis: licensed cybersecurity providers (Promoter, Tier 3) by 31 December 2026; CII auditors (Advocate, Tier 5) by end 2026; and CII owners (Advocate, Tier 5) by end 2027. The requirements cover licensed penetration-testing services and managed SOC monitoring.

The CSA has concluded its public consultation on cybersecurity licensing framework updates, with key changes including mandatory certification requirements, extended five-year licence validity and streamlined notification processes, expected to come into force by end-2027.

Compliance Alert
