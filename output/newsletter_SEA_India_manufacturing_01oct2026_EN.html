Policy Focus

Regulation, certification and tariff changes affecting consumer electronics

🇮🇳 India — BIS QCO for 90+ electrical appliance categories takes effect 1 October 2026

India's Safety of Household, Commercial and Similar Electrical Appliances (Quality Control) Order, 2026 requires more than 90 categories of household, commercial and industrial electrical appliances to obtain BIS certification and bear the ISI mark prior to market entry in India, effective 1 October 2026. Covered products include shavers, trimmers, toothbrushes, blenders and air appliances. For foreign manufacturers, certification is a precondition for market access.

Status: In force from 01 October 2026
⚠️ Non-compliance can result in import restrictions, product seizures and financial penalties.
2026 · India Briefing · source

🇮🇩 Indonesia — MOT Reg 19/2026 reshapes e-commerce and local-content enforcement

Minister of Trade Regulation No. 19 of 2026 replaces MOT Reg 31/2023, expanding the scope of businesses covered, adding obligations for online platforms and merchants, tightening conditions for cross-border sellers, and establishing Indonesia's first trade-level framework for artificial intelligence in commerce. The regulation emphasises platform accountability, seller verification, product transparency and domestic product visibility. TKDN local content rules require minimum locally-sourced component percentages for foreign brands selling electronics in Indonesia.

Status: In effect; platforms carry direct enforcement responsibilities
⚠️ Devices without complete TKDN certification risk IMEI blocking on Telkomsel, Indosat, XL and Tri networks, and delisting from Tokopedia, Shopee, Lazada and Blibli.
14 Jul 2026 · Dentons HPRP · source

🇲🇾 Malaysia — MCMC communication equipment standards enter full enforcement 5 November 2026

Malaysia's MCMC has set implementation dates for two communication equipment standards, effective from 5 May 2026 with a six-month grace period. All subsequent related equipment submitted for certification must comply with the two new standards, with full enforcement starting 5 November 2026. Sellers must ensure products are certified and labelled by MCMC, with non-compliance carrying severe penalties.

Status: Grace period running; full enforcement 05 November 2026
⚠️ Online shopping platforms are required to verify SIRIM certification for listed equipment.
2026 · GMA CAICT · source

🇸🇬 Singapore — Residential gateways move to RG-SEC Issue 2 and CLS Level 2

Singapore's IMDA is transitioning residential gateways to RG-SEC Issue 2 and CLS Level 2 cybersecurity requirements, with new-model registration changing on 8 December 2026 and full local-market compliance required by 31 December 2027. Separately, CSA and IMDA plan to raise the mandatory cybersecurity certification requirement for household routers from CLS Level 1 to Level 2 by end-2027.

Status: New-model registration change 08 December 2026; full compliance by 31 December 2027
2026 · ICERTIFI / CSA · source

🇺🇸→🇦🇺 US Section 301 forced-labour duties apply to ASEAN-origin electronics

For covered merchandise entered on or after 24 July 2026, Cambodia, Indonesia and Malaysia are subject to an additional 10% Section 301 duty, while the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand and Vietnam are subject to an additional 12.5% duty. Brunei Darussalam, Lao PDR, Myanmar and Timor-Leste are treated separately under the action.

BREAKINGPolicy

Indonesia's MOT Reg 19/2026 tightens e-commerce rules and domestic product visibility

Minister of Trade Regulation No. 19 of 2026 replaces MOT Reg 31/2023, expanding the categories of businesses subject to PMSE rules, adding mandatory protections for sellers, introducing algorithmic obligations for marketplace platforms, tightening conditions for cross-border sellers, and establishing Indonesia's first trade-level framework for artificial intelligence in commerce.

The regulation emphasises platform accountability, seller verification, product transparency and domestic product visibility. Platforms now carry direct enforcement responsibilities, and multinational companies, foreign investors and cross-border sellers must reassess existing PMSE operations against the new requirements.

Compliance Alert

Cross-border electronics sellers face tighter listing conditions and domestic-product prioritisation on Indonesian marketplaces.

14 Jul 2026 · Dentons HPRP · source
Policy

TKDN local content rules gate electronics market access and network activation

TKDN (Tingkat Komponen Dalam Negeri) local content requirements mandate that certain product categories contain a minimum percentage of locally-sourced components for foreign brands selling electronics in Indonesia. Operators Telkomsel, Indosat, XL and Tri block IMEIs of devices without complete certification, preventing use of Indonesian SIM cards.

E-commerce platforms including Tokopedia, Shopee, Lazada and Blibli increasingly require proof of TKDN before listing. If devices are found being sold without proper TKDN, customs and tax authorities can demand reclamation of preferential duties.

Compliance Alert

Three enforcement layers — network IMEI blocking, marketplace listing requirements and duty clawback — make TKDN a hard gate rather than a soft preference.

2026 · Kickrate · source

Philippines faces 12.5% US Section 301 duty but retains ATIGA zero-tariff access

The Philippines is subject to an additional 12.5% US Section 301 forced-labour duty on covered merchandise entered on or after 24 July 2026. However, under the ASEAN Trade in Goods Agreement (ATIGA), the Philippines has eliminated tariffs on approximately 99% of all goods from ASEAN trading partners, including Vietnam and Thailand, provided correct documentation is in place.

The Philippines also maintains a de minimis threshold of PHP 10,000 FOB value per consignment, below which both customs duty and VAT are exempt.

India's BIS QCO for 90+ electrical appliance categories takes effect 1 October 2026

India's Safety of Household, Commercial and Similar Electrical Appliances (Quality Control) Order, 2026 requires more than 90 categories of household, commercial and industrial electrical appliances to obtain BIS certification and bear the ISI mark prior to market entry in India, effective 1 October 2026. Covered products include shavers, trimmers, toothbrushes, blenders and air appliances.

Policy

PLI scheme generates ₹7.5 lakh crore production and 14.39 lakh jobs

As of 2026, India's Production Linked Incentive scheme has generated ₹7.5 lakh crore in production, attracted ₹3.2 lakh crore in investments and created 14.39 lakh jobs. First introduced in April 2020 for mobile phone and electronics manufacturing, the scheme later expanded to 14 sectors and is administered by the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade.

Despite the incentives, a large percentage of key components for mobile and electronics manufacturing are still imported, indicating continued reliance on foreign suppliers. The Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme (ECMS) offers 5–10% on qualifying production for 5–8 years, with higher incentives for capital-intensive, high-difficulty components.

Strategic Read

ECMS is building the component supply chain beneath the assembly capacity PLI created — lower competition and longer incentive windows than the original PLI.

India PCB market grows on Make in India and supply chain diversification

India's PCB industry is at an inflection point, driven by government initiatives including Make in India and PLI schemes, combined with global supply chain diversification away from China. The market is estimated at approximately $4–5 billion, with major players including AT&S India, Tata Electronics and Foxconn.

Significant demand comes from smartphone, automotive electronics and defence sectors, with calls to increase government incentives for semiconductor and PCB fabrication in India.

India Semiconductor Mission backs 28nm fab and Tata OSAT in Assam

India's semiconductor programme targets 28nm and above mature-node production on 300mm wafers, with a target capacity of 50,000 wafer starts per month at full ramp and an investment of Rs 91,000 crore (approximately $11 billion) with India Semiconductor Mission support. Target markets are automotive ICs, power management, display drivers, microcontrollers and IoT devices.

The India Semiconductor Mission is supporting initiatives including Tata Electronics' OSAT facility in Jagiroad, Assam, focusing on mature-node chip packaging and test. The Union cabinet approved Semicon 2.0 with an allocation of INR 1.275 trillion (US$13.23 billion), announced on 15 July 2026, spanning chip design, fabrication, display manufacturing, advanced packaging, semiconductor equipment, speciality materials, R&D and talent development.
