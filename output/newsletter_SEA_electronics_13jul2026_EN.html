🌏 Global Industry Shifts — SEA Impact

Chinese Appliance Brands Gain Ground in SEA

Chinese home appliance brands are gaining ground in Southeast Asia, with market share rising over the past five years as they attract consumers with services such as round-the-clock support and innovative design, moving beyond competitive pricing. In Malaysia, the home appliance market was once dominated by big Japanese names.

→ Downstream SEA impact: Intensifying competition for Japanese and Korean incumbents across Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand, and Vietnam.
Source: KrASIA / Nikkei Asia, 30 Jun 2026

Chinese Consumer Brands Surge Across ASEAN

Chinese consumer brands are rapidly expanding across Southeast Asia, moving beyond electronics and EVs into beauty, food service, and home appliances, per Euromonitor. ASEAN economies of Indonesia, Malaysia, Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, and Vietnam account for 95% of the region's $4 trillion GDP.

→ Downstream SEA impact: Chinese brands challenging legacy Japanese, Korean, and European players across multiple consumer categories.
Source: China Daily, 22 May 2026

Samsung Is the Only Top 5 Smartphone Brand Growing in SEA

Omdia reports Samsung is the only top 5 smartphone brand growing in Southeast Asia in Q1 2026. OPPO second with 4.2M units (20% share, -17% YoY), Xiaomi third with 3.7M units (17% share, -12% YoY), TRANSSION fourth with 3.4M units (-10% YoY).

→ Downstream SEA impact: Market consolidation favors Samsung; Chinese brands under margin pressure.
Source: Android Headlines / Omdia, May 2026

SEA Smartphone Shipments Fall 9% in 1Q26; ASP Hits Record $349

Omdia reports Southeast Asia smartphone shipments totaled 21.6M units in 1Q26, down 9% YoY. Average selling price reached a record high of $349, up 19% YoY, driven by memory cost inflation and vendor repricing. Xiaomi raised Redmi Note 15 4G price to RM799 from RM699 in Malaysia.

→ Downstream SEA impact: Volume decline with value growth signals structural shift toward premiumization.
Source: Omdia, May 2026

China+1 Strategy Recalibrates SEA Supply Chains

Southeast Asia's consumer electronics export sector, valued at over $145B and projected to grow at 7.2% CAGR through 2030, is a direct consequence of the China+1 strategy adopted by global brands diversifying away from geopolitical risk.

→ Downstream SEA impact: Vietnam, Malaysia, Thailand, and Indonesia primary beneficiaries of manufacturing relocation.
Source: Alibaba.com Seller Blog, 2026

📜 Policy Focus

🇸🇬 Singapore: MELS Labeling Mandatory from 1 July 2026

Singapore's NEA implemented mandatory energy efficiency compliance (MELS) for key household appliances from 1 July 2026, requiring MEPS compliance and NEA registration for ACs, fridges, dryers, TVs, and lighting.

Effective: 1 July 2026

🇲🇾 Malaysia: SIRIM Certification & LVG Sales Tax from May 2026

Effective 1 May 2026, Malaysia mandates SIRIM certification and local representative registration for electronic, electrical, and mechanical equipment imports. A 10% sales tax applies on cross-border direct shipments valued under MYR 500.

Effective: 1 May 2026
⚠️ Compliance Alert: International exporters and e-commerce platforms must ensure SIRIM certification for all covered electronics.

🇮🇩 Indonesia: TKDN Local Content Enforcement & New E-Commerce Regulation

Indonesia's TKDN regulation requires electronics to have local content; non-compliant devices cannot be activated on Indonesian networks (IMEI blocking) or sold legally on e-commerce platforms. Permendag 19/2026 strengthens e-commerce rules including seller licensing, cross-border trade restrictions, and AI governance.

Enforcement: Ongoing
⚠️ Compliance Alert: Foreign brands must obtain TKDN certification for all devices sold in Indonesia.

🇸🇬 Singapore: CSA Cybersecurity Labelling Scheme (CLS) for IoT

Singapore's CSA CLS is voluntary for most consumer IoT devices but mandatory for Wi-Fi routers (Level 1). Four-level rating system from self-declaration to penetration testing. Mutual recognition agreements exist with Finland, Germany, UK, and South Korea.

Effective: Ongoing

🇺🇸 US Tariff Impact on SEA: Section 122 Expiry & Vietnam/Malaysia Frameworks

The 10% Section 122 tariff on electronics from China is set to expire on 24 July 2026. Vietnam electronics face only the 10% Section 122 rate. The US signed framework agreements with Vietnam (20% reciprocal rate, potential 0% on some articles) and Malaysia (19% reciprocal rate).

Key Date: 24 July 2026 (Section 122 expiry)
⚠️ Sourcing Implication: Vietnam and Malaysia gain tariff advantage over China for US-bound electronics.

Indonesia

Smartphones · TKDN · Home Appliances
Policy

Indonesia TKDN: Non-Compliant Devices Blocked on Networks

Indonesia's TKDN regulation requires electronics to have local content. Operators (Telkomsel, Indosat, XL, Tri) block IMEIs of devices without complete certification. E-commerce platforms (Tokopedia, Shopee, Lazada, Blibli) increasingly require proof of TKDN before listing.

New regulation introduces filtering mechanism for goods not eligible for certification; only goods produced from industrial activities in Indonesia with proper KBLI can be certified.

Compliance Alert

Foreign brands must obtain TKDN certification for all devices; risk of IMEI blocking and sales prohibition.

2026 · Kickrate · Kickrate
Policy

Indonesia Permendag 19/2026: New E-Commerce Regulation

Minister of Trade Regulation No. 19 of 2026 expands categories of businesses subject to PMSE rules, adds mandatory protections for sellers, introduces algorithmic obligations for marketplace platforms, tightens conditions for cross-border sellers, and establishes Indonesia's first trade-level framework for AI in commerce.

Platforms now carry direct enforcement responsibilities. Multinational companies and foreign investors must reassess PMSE operations.

Compliance Alert

Cross-border sellers face tighter conditions; AI governance requirements for marketplace platforms.
