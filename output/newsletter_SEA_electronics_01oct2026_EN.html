Global Industry Shifts

Only developments with a named, direct SEA-country impact

China+1 momentum keeps SEA EMS market on a 10% growth track

Southeast Asia's electronics manufacturing services market is estimated at USD 28.91 billion in 2026, up from USD 26.03 billion in 2025, and is projected to reach USD 46.59 billion by 2031 at a 10.01% CAGR. The China+1 diversification push, foreign direct investment and government incentive packages are cited as the primary drivers elevating ASEAN as a preferred production base.

Vietnam is described as well-positioned to capture diversification demand, scaling electronics assembly and component manufacturing and climbing into semiconductors and PCB production. Indonesia is flagged as attractive on population, labour cost and middle-class growth, while Malaysia is characterised as a higher-tech, specialised manufacturing location rather than a large-scale volume play.

→ Downstream SEA impact: Vietnam and Indonesia absorb the bulk of volume relocation; Malaysia's role narrows toward specialised, higher-value work.
2026 · Mordor Intelligence · Southeast Asia Electronics Manufacturing Services Market

Chinese brands reshape SEA consumer preferences across electronics

Euromonitor reports that Chinese brands are reshaping Southeast Asia's consumer landscape, with Chinese products increasingly valued for high quality, innovation and value in sectors including electronics. Separately, China leads the top 50 electronics and appliances brands in 2026 with 19 brands, ahead of Japan with 12 and the US with 9.

The shift matters for category teams because brand-origin preference in SEA is no longer purely a price story — quality and innovation perceptions are moving in Chinese brands' favour across the region's consumer electronics aisles.

→ Downstream SEA impact: Assortment and brand-portfolio decisions across SEA retail should reflect rising Chinese-brand equity rather than treating them as price-tier only.
2026 · Euromonitor · How Chinese Brands Are Transforming Southeast Asia's Markets

Global smartphone market declines 7% YoY in Q2 2026 on memory shortage

The global smartphone market declined 7% year-on-year in Q2 2026 as a memory shortage deepened and memory prices continued to rise. Some OEMs frontloaded shipments in anticipation of further component price hikes and logistics cost escalation, producing a milder-than-expected decline. Developed regions proved more resilient due to greater premium-segment exposure.

IDC's Q2 2026 worldwide data shows Samsung at 62.7 million units (22.7% share, +8.1% YoY) and Apple at 55.7 million units (20.2%, +14.9%), while Xiaomi fell 26.4%, OPPO 17.1% and vivo 19.6%.

→ Downstream SEA impact: Memory-driven BOM inflation and frontloaded shipments will shape SEA channel inventory and pricing discipline into the next quarters.

Core Component Supply Chain Update

PCB/FPC · Display · Camera · Battery · Passive · Compressors/Motors

PCB / FPC — Vietnam flexible-circuit base anchors SEA board supply

Vietnam's PCB manufacturing base spans flexible printed circuits (FPCs), wiring harnesses and rigid boards. Fujikura Vietnam specialises in FPCs, wiring harnesses and electronic components for automotive, telecommunications and consumer electronics, with facilities emphasising precision manufacturing and stable quality control for mass production. Mektec Vietnam is also cited among high-quality flexible-circuit specialists for compact, high-performance devices, while Vector Fabrication Vietnam produces PCBs for aerospace, consumer electronics and specialised medical applications.

The Southeast Asia printed circuit board market is forecast to reach USD 10.12 billion by 2031, with flexible circuits leading growth on demand from wearables, foldable phones and vehicle-display adoption.

📍 Vietnam (Fujikura, Mektec, Vector, Jing Gong, Sunching) · status: established, expanding

Sourcing implication: FPC capacity in Vietnam is deep enough to support foldable and wearable ramps — qualify dual sources across Fujikura and Mektec for premium flexible builds.

Display — Samsung Display's Vietnam OLED plant underpins regional panel supply

Samsung Display is building a USD 1.8 billion OLED plant in northern Vietnam's Yen Phong industrial park, Bac Ninh province, to produce OLED displays for automobiles and technology equipment, located close to an existing Samsung Electronics plant. The company has also announced plans to invest 4.1 trillion Korean won in the IT OLED field by 2026.

At IMID 2026 in Busan, Samsung Display exhibited a wide-view foldable panel and a stretchable display, while LG Display unveiled FLiPP, a mask-free OLED manufacturing process. LG Display has separately put its Guangzhou LCD plant up for sale as it exits the LCD business.

📍 Vietnam (Bac Ninh) / Korea · status: capacity build-out, technology showcase

Sourcing implication: Northern Vietnam OLED capacity strengthens local panel availability for automotive and IT displays — relevant for SEA device programmes seeking non-China panel origin.

Camera modules — LG Innotek's Vietnam plant targets up to 80% of output

LG Innotek has completed construction of its new camera module factory in Haiphong, Vietnam, which has started operations and aims to double the company's global camera module production capacity. The company plans to spend 1.3 trillion won on the facility and intends for Vietnam to account for up to 80% of its total camera module output within two to three years.

Under its multichannel strategy, LG Innotek will use its Gumi plant as a "mother factory" for R&D and high-value-added modules for new models, with Paju handling advanced camera modules and 3D sensing. The Vietnam subsidiary serves volume production, giving an edge on price amid competition for smartphone orders.

📍 Vietnam (Haiphong) · status: operational, capacity doubling

Sourcing implication: Camera-module pricing pressure should ease for SEA-assembled handsets as Haiphong volume scales — revisit camera BOM quotes at next RFQ cycle.

Passive — MLCC tightness persists as AI demand crowds out consumer supply

The MLCC market is dominated by Murata at roughly 40% global share and Samsung Electro-Mechanics at around 18%. Together they control about 80–85% of AI-grade MLCCs. TDK holds ~5%, Taiyo Yuden ~4% and all others ~7%. Murata has increased prices for certain high-capacitance AI-related MLCC products by 15% to 35% in 2026, and Yageo implemented price increases across MLCC, resistor and inductor lines starting 1 July 2026.

Capacity is being prioritised for high-value, high-capacitance products used in AI servers, data centres and advanced computing, creating supply pressure for industrial, telecom, automotive and consumer device segments. Murata's Philippines site lowers exposure to Japanese seismic risk, and Samsung's Philippine plant doubles automotive line throughput.

📍 Philippines (Murata, Samsung Electro-Mechanics) · status: tight, price increases in effect

Sourcing implication: Consumer-device MLCC allocation is being squeezed by AI-grade demand — lock lead times and consider design substitutions on non-critical capacitance.

Compressors / Motors — Nidec and Embraco push efficiency into appliance lines

Nidec Global Appliance and Embraco showcased their latest technologies at AWE 2026, including Nidec motor technologies for home appliances and Embraco's VESJ compressor prototype, the fifth generation of the flagship VES family, designed for low noise and energy efficiency to support "A-level" regulatory ratings. Embraco also highlighted Integrated Control Boards (ICBs) that control not only the compressor but the entire refrigerator.

Nidec's portfolio includes energy-efficient BLDC motors for washing machines and the Direct Drive motor DD124 Gen 3 for washers. Nidec's VLT variable-speed compressor supplies compressors for AC and refrigeration in Thailand and other countries. Nidec Global Appliance has a production capacity of 70 million units of motors and compressors per year across 9 countries.

📍 Thailand (supply) / global manufacturing · status: technology showcase, capacity in place

Sourcing implication: Efficiency-led compressor and motor platforms give SEA appliance makers a route to meet tightening MEPS — evaluate VESJ and DD124 Gen 3 for next-generation SKUs.

Battery — ATL remains the consumer-cell benchmark; no new SEA plant news

Amperex Technology Limited (ATL) is a world-leading manufacturer of lithium-ion batteries, specialising in rechargeable cells, packs and system integration, with products integrated into smartphones, laptops, tablets, smartwatches, Bluetooth headsets, AR/VR systems, smart door locks and power banks. The company is headquartered in Hong Kong with subsidiaries in China.

Sunwoda's ESS business is growing faster than its EV business, and its Thailand plant is designed to serve Southeast Asian EV production. No new ATL, Amperex or Sunwoda consumer-cell factory announcements for Malaysia or Vietnam were reported this week.

📍 Hong Kong / China (ATL); Thailand (Sunwoda, EV-oriented) · status: no new SEA consumer-cell capacity this week

Sourcing implication: Consumer-cell supply for SEA device programmes remains anchored outside the region — no near-term localisation relief on battery BOM.

Policy Focus

Regulatory changes affecting consumer electronics across SEA

🇮🇩 Indonesia — MOT Regulation No. 19 of 2026 rewrites e-commerce rules

Indonesia's Ministry of Trade has issued Regulation No. 19 of 2026 on the Implementation of E-Commerce Activities, replacing MOT Regulation No. 31 of 2023. The new regulation expands the categories of businesses subject to PMSE rules, adds mandatory protections for sellers, introduces algorithmic obligations for marketplace platforms, tightens conditions for cross-border sellers, and establishes Indonesia's first trade-level framework for artificial intelligence in commerce. Platforms now carry direct enforcement responsibilities.

The regulation emphasises licensing compliance, consumer protection and the promotion of domestic products. For consumer electronics sellers, the tightening of cross-border seller conditions and platform enforcement duties is the operative change.

Status: In force — MOT Reg. 19/2026 replaces MOT Reg. 31/2023
⚠️ Cross-border electronics sellers on Tokopedia, Shopee, Lazada and Blibli should re-verify licensing and platform obligations under the new framework.

🇮🇩 Indonesia — TKDN local content enforcement carries device-level penalties

TKDN (Tingkat Komponen Dalam Negeri) local content requirements for electronics are mandatory, with penalties for non-compliance including inability to activate devices on Indonesian networks. Operators Telkomsel, Indosat, XL and Tri block IMEIs of devices without complete certification, meaning customers cannot use Indonesian SIM cards. E-commerce platforms increasingly require proof of TKDN before listing, and customs and tax authorities can demand reclamation of preferential duties if devices are found being sold without proper TKDN.

The enforcement stack — IMEI blocking, listing gating and duty clawback — makes TKDN compliance a hard gate rather than a paperwork formality for handsets entering Indonesia.

Status: Mandatory — IMEI blocking and listing gating active
⚠️ Devices without complete TKDN certification cannot be activated on Indonesian networks and risk tax/duty clawback.

🇲🇾 Malaysia — MCMC sets 5 November 2026 full enforcement for two equipment standards

On 6 July 2026, the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission announced the implementation of two new communication equipment standards, with a six-month grace period until full enforcement on 5 November 2026. All subsequent related equipment submitted for certification must comply with the two new standards, and the government encourages applicants to achieve compliance during the grace period.

SIRIM certification remains mandatory for a wide range of electrical, electronic and multimedia products to ensure safety and interoperability, with communication, multimedia and hybrid products regulated in collaboration with MCMC. The certificate holder must be a local business entity registered with SSM and must possess suitable premises such as a registered office or warehouse.

Status: Grace period — full enforcement 5 November 2026
⚠️ Certification submissions filed after the grace period must meet the two new standards; local entity and premises requirements still apply.

🇸🇬 Singapore — IMDA shifts residential gateways to RG-SEC Issue 2 and CLS Level 2

Singapore's IMDA is transitioning residential gateways to RG-SEC Issue 2 and CLS Level 2 cybersecurity requirements, with new-model registration changing on 8 December 2026 and full local-market compliance required by 31 December 2027. The CSA and IMDA announced in March 2026 that mandatory cybersecurity requirements for residential routers would rise from Cybersecurity Labelling Scheme Level 1 to Level 2 by the end of 2027.

The CSA operates its own certification marks, Cyber Essentials and Cyber Trust, under the SG Cyber Safe programme. The Cybersecurity Labelling Scheme is a voluntary programme assessing and labelling consumer IoT devices based on security features. No local testing is required for IoT devices in Singapore.

Status: Transition — new-model registration 8 December 2026; full compliance 31 December 2027

🇻🇳 Vietnam — QCVN 31:2026/BXD electric motorcycle battery rules take effect 9 October 2026

Vietnam has released new regulations for electric motorcycle batteries, QCVN 31:2026/BXD, which take effect on 9 October 2026. The regulation adds a compliance requirement for battery systems in the Vietnamese two-wheeler segment.

While the rule targets electric motorcycle batteries rather than consumer electronics cells directly, it signals Vietnam's continued tightening of battery safety and conformity requirements — a direction relevant to any battery-containing consumer device entering the market.

Status: Effective 9 October 2026

🇵🇭 Philippines — ATIGA zero tariffs and PHP 10,000 de minimis threshold

The ASEAN Trade in Goods Agreement provides zero or near-zero tariff rates on goods originating in any of the 10 ASEAN member states, including Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia and Indonesia, provided the correct documentation is in place. Under the Common Effective Preferential Tariff scheme, the Philippines has eliminated tariffs on approximately 99% of all goods from ASEAN trading partners. The Philippines also maintains a de minimis threshold of PHP 10,000 FOB value per consignment, below which both customs duty and VAT are exempt.

For non-ASEAN-origin electronics, the Section 301 forced-labour tariff framework applies at 12.5% for Vietnam, Thailand and the Philippines, with Malaysia at 13.4% and Thailand at 15.9% under the broader electronics tariff schedule.

Status: ATIGA preferences active; de minimis PHP 10,000 FOB per consignment
