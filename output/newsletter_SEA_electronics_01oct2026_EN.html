Global Industry Shifts

Cross-border moves with a named, direct Southeast Asia impact

China+1 Consolidates Around Vietnam and Indonesia as SEA EMS Market Hits USD 28.91bn

Southeast Asia's electronics manufacturing services market is estimated at USD 28.91 billion in 2026, up from USD 26.03 billion in 2025, and is projected to reach USD 46.59 billion by 2031 at a 10.01% CAGR. The China+1 diversification push, expanding foreign direct investment and government incentive packages are named as the primary drivers elevating ASEAN as a preferred production base.

Vietnam is positioned to capture the bulk of diversification demand, having scaled electronics assembly and component manufacturing over the past 15 years and now climbing into semiconductors and PCB production. Indonesia is flagged as attractive on population, labour cost and middle-class growth, while Malaysia is characterised as a higher-tech and specialised manufacturing location rather than a large-scale volume play.

→ Downstream SEA impact: Vietnam and Indonesia absorb volume assembly; Malaysia's EMS base must compete on advanced packaging, photonics and specialised work rather than scale.

Chinese Brands Reshape SEA Consumer Landscape as Global Brand League Table Tilts

Euromonitor reports that Chinese brands are reshaping Southeast Asia's consumer landscape, with Chinese products increasingly valued for quality, innovation and value in electronics. Separately compiled data shows China leading the top 50 electronics and appliances brands in 2026 with 19 brands, ahead of Japan with 12 and the US with 9.

The shift is visible in category-level share: in Malaysia, a market once dominated by big Japanese names, Chinese appliance brands have gained ground over the past five years, moving beyond competitive pricing into round-the-clock support and design-led differentiation.

→ Downstream SEA impact: Japanese and Korean incumbents face share erosion in Malaysia, Thailand and the Philippines; channel partners should reassess brand mix and after-sales commitments.

Global Smartphone Market Down 7% YoY in Q2 2026 as Memory Shortage Bites

The global smartphone market declined 7% year-on-year in Q2 2026 as the memory shortage deepened and memory prices continued to rise. Some OEMs frontloaded shipments in anticipation of further component price hikes and logistics cost escalation, producing a milder-than-expected decline.

Developed regions proved more resilient due to greater exposure to the premium segment, which is better positioned to absorb component inflation. IDC's Q2 2026 worldwide table shows Samsung at 22.7% share (+8.1% YoY) and Apple at 20.2% (+14.9%), while Xiaomi fell 26.4%, OPPO 17.1% and vivo 19.6%.

→ Downstream SEA impact: SEA is a mid-tier-heavy region, so memory-driven BOM inflation hits local price bands hardest — expect further ASP increases and thinner entry-level line-ups.

PCB / FPC — Vietnam Flex Circuit Base Anchors Wearable and Foldable Demand

Vietnam's PCB manufacturing base continues to specialise in flexible printed circuits for compact, high-performance devices. Fujikura Vietnam produces FPCs, wiring harnesses and electronic components for automotive, telecommunications and consumer electronics, while Mektec Vietnam focuses on high-quality flexible circuits. Jing Gong Electronics covers a broader material range including aluminium PCBs, and Sunching Electronics Vietnam has operated in Bac Ninh since October 2001.

The Southeast Asia printed circuit board market is forecast to reach USD 10.12 billion by 2031, with flexible circuits leading growth on demand from wearables, foldable phones and vehicle-display adoption. Recent capacity additions include Zhen Ding Technology's USD 2 billion Rayong plant entering trial production in January 2026 with 1.2 million m² of any-layer HDI capacity, and Meiko Electronics completing a USD 200 million multilayer-board factory in Vietnam in December 2025 with 600,000 m² of annual capacity.

📍 Vietnam (Bac Ninh, Bac Giang) · Thailand (Rayong) · Status: capacity expanding, FPC-led

Sourcing implication: FPC supply for foldables and wearables is increasingly Vietnam-resident — dual-source qualification across Fujikura and Mektec Vietnam reduces single-site exposure ahead of 2027 foldable ramp.

Passive Components — MLCC Market Splits K-Shaped as AI Demand Diverts Capacity

The MLCC market has entered a K-shaped recovery in 2026. Murata holds approximately 40% of the global MLCC market and Samsung Electro-Mechanics around 18%; together they control roughly 80–85% of AI-grade MLCCs. Murata has increased prices for certain high-capacitance AI-related MLCC products by 15% to 35% in 2026, and Yageo implemented price increases across MLCC, resistor and inductor lines from 1 July 2026.

Capacity is being prioritised for high-value, high-capacitance products used in AI servers, data centres and advanced computing, creating supply pressure for industrial equipment, telecommunications, automotive electronics and consumer devices. On geographic diversification, Murata's Philippines site lowers exposure to Japanese seismic risk, Samsung's Philippine plant doubles automotive line throughput, and Taiyo Yuden is building a USD 200 million dielectric research campus targeting sub-0.4 µm layers for a 30% capacitance uplift by 2028.

📍 Philippines (Murata, Samsung Electro-Mechanics) · Japan · Taiwan · Status: tight, price-increasing

Compliance alert for procurement: consumer-device MLCC allocation is being crowded out by AI-grade demand — lock 2027 volume commitments early and budget for mid-single-digit to double-digit price escalation on high-capacitance parts.

Compressors & Motors — Nidec and Embraco Push Efficiency Platforms Into Appliance Programmes

Nidec Global Appliance and Embraco showcased motor and compressor technologies at AWE 2026, including Nidec's energy-efficient BLDC motors for washing machines, the Direct Drive motor DD124 Gen 3 for washers, and Embraco's VESJ compressor prototype — the fifth generation of the flagship VES family, designed for low noise and energy efficiency to support "A-level" ratings under strict regulatory standards. Embraco also highlighted Integrated Control Boards that control the entire refrigerator, not just the compressor.

Nidec Global Appliance operates with a production capacity of 70 million units of motors and compressors per year, a sales presence in more than 80 countries, over 15,000 employees across 9 countries and more than 50 R&D laboratories. Embraco's variable-speed compressor line supplies AC and refrigeration applications in Thailand and other countries.

📍 Thailand (supply) · Global (AWE 2026) · Status: platform refresh, efficiency-led

Market signal: Efficiency-rated compressor and motor platforms are the lever for meeting tightening MEPS across SEA — appliance OEMs should align 2027 model roadmaps to VES-class and BLDC platforms now.

Policy Focus

Regulation, certification and tariff changes affecting device and appliance placement

🇮🇩 Indonesia — MOT Regulation No. 19 of 2026 Rewrites E-Commerce Rules

The Ministry of Trade has issued Regulation No. 19 of 2026 on the Implementation of E-Commerce Activities, replacing MOT Regulation No. 31 of 2023. The new framework expands the categories of businesses subject to PMSE rules, adds mandatory protections for sellers, introduces algorithmic obligations for marketplace platforms, tightens conditions for cross-border sellers, and establishes Indonesia's first trade-level framework for artificial intelligence in commerce. Platforms now carry direct enforcement responsibilities.

Alongside the e-commerce update, TKDN local content requirements for electronics remain mandatory. Non-compliant devices face IMEI blocking by operators Telkomsel, Indosat, XL and Tri — meaning customers cannot use Indonesian SIM cards — and marketplaces including Tokopedia, Shopee, Lazada and Blibli increasingly require proof of TKDN before listing. Customs and tax authorities can also demand reclamation of previously granted preferential treatment.

Status: In force — MOT Reg. 19/2026 replaces MOT Reg. 31/2023; TKDN enforcement active across operators and marketplaces
⚠️ Compliance alert: Cross-border sellers listing on Indonesian marketplaces now face both platform-level TKDN verification and expanded PMSE licensing scope. Verify IMEI registration and TKDN documentation before any Q4 listing push.

🇲🇾 Malaysia — MCMC Sets 5 November 2026 Full Enforcement for Two New Equipment Standards

MCMC announced the implementation of two new communication equipment standards with a six-month grace period, moving to full enforcement on 5 November 2026. All subsequent related equipment submitted for certification must comply with the new standards, and the government is encouraging applicants to achieve compliance during the grace period.

SIRIM certification remains mandatory across a wide range of electrical, electronic and multimedia products to ensure safety and interoperability, with communication, multimedia and hybrid products regulated in conjunction with MCMC. The certificate holder must be a local business entity registered with SSM and must possess suitable premises such as a registered office or warehouse to store and distribute certified goods. Malaysia's Digital (MD) Status continues to offer incentives for digital, ICT, software, AI and multimedia companies, including tax incentives and exemptions for ICT equipment.

Status: Grace period running — full enforcement 5 November 2026
⚠️ Compliance alert: Any device model not yet submitted under the two new standards has roughly five weeks of grace remaining. Local entity and premises requirements mean certification cannot be run from offshore.

🇸🇬 Singapore — IMDA Shifts Residential Gateways to RG-SEC Issue 2 and CLS Level 2

IMDA is transitioning residential gateways to RG-SEC Issue 2 and CLS Level 2 cybersecurity requirements, with new-model registration changing on 8 December 2026 and full local-market compliance required by 31 December 2027. The Cyber Security Agency of Singapore and IMDA announced in March 2026 that mandatory cybersecurity requirements for residential routers would rise from Cybersecurity Labelling Scheme Level 1 to Level 2 by end-2027.

CSA operates its own certification marks — Cyber Essentials and Cyber Trust — under the SG Cyber Safe programme, alongside IMDA and PDPC anchors for cloud security (MTCS SS 584) and data protection (Data Protection Trustmark, now Singapore Standard SS 714:2025). CSA has also concluded its public consultation on cybersecurity licensing framework updates, with key changes including mandatory certification requirements, extended five-year licence validity and streamlined notification processes, expected to come into force by end-2027.

Status: Transition announced — new-model registration changes 8 December 2026; full compliance by 31 December 2027

🇻🇳 🇹🇭 🇵🇭 — Tariff Landscape: 12.5% US Rate on Vietnam, Thailand and Philippines Electronics

US import tariffs on electronics stand at a 12.5% effective rate for Vietnam, Thailand and the Philippines, with Malaysia at 13.4% and Cambodia and Indonesia at 10%. China remains at a 37.5% effective rate. For intra-ASEAN trade, the ASEAN Trade in Goods Agreement provides zero or near-zero tariff rates on goods originating in any of the 10 member states, with the Philippines having eliminated tariffs on approximately 99% of all goods from ASEAN trading partners under the Common Effective Preferential Tariff scheme.

The Philippines maintains a de minimis threshold of PHP 10,000 FOB value per consignment, below which both customs duty and VAT are exempt. On origin, US Customs and Border Protection is scrutinising whether Chinese components assembled in Vietnam represent a genuine change of origin rather than tariff routing, and Vietnam's trade agreement specifically targets transshipment.

Status: In force — ATIGA preferences require correct documentation; US origin scrutiny intensifying
⚠️ Compliance alert: ATIGA zero-tariff treatment is documentation-dependent. Origin substantiation for Vietnam-assembled goods using Chinese components is now a live audit risk on both the US and intra-ASEAN lanes.

Indonesia's MOT Reg. 19/2026 Expands E-Commerce Scope and Tightens Cross-Border Seller Conditions

The Ministry of Trade issued Regulation No. 19 of 2026 on the Implementation of E-Commerce Activities, replacing MOT Regulation No. 31 of 2023. The regulation expands the categories of businesses subject to PMSE rules, adds mandatory protections for sellers, introduces algorithmic obligations for marketplace platforms, tightens conditions for cross-border sellers, and establishes Indonesia's first trade-level framework for artificial intelligence in commerce. The regulation responds to the rapid evolution of Indonesia's digital economy by expanding regulatory coverage, strengthening consumer protection and reinforcing promotion of domestic products. TKDN Non-Compliance Triggers IMEI Blocking and Tax Clawback Risk for Electronics

TKDN local content requirements for electronics remain mandatory in Indonesia. Operators Telkomsel, Indosat, XL and Tri block IMEIs of devices without complete certification, meaning customers cannot use Indonesian SIM cards. E-commerce platforms including Tokopedia, Shopee, Lazada and Blibli increasingly require proof of TKDN before listing, and customs and tax authorities can demand reclamation of previously granted preferential treatment if devices are found being sold without proper TKDN.

The enforcement stack is therefore three-layered: network-level IMEI blocking, marketplace-level listing verification, and retrospective fiscal clawback. Each layer operates independently, so clearing one does not imply clearance of the others.

Compliance Alert

The clawback provision is the material risk — it reaches backwards into already-shipped inventory. Any Indonesia-bound device programme should treat TKDN certification as a pre-shipment gate, not a post-landing formality.
