Global Industry Shifts

Chinese Appliance Brands Extend SEA Lead

Chinese consumer brands are accelerating expansion in Southeast Asia by acquiring rivals and leveraging e-commerce, pushing deeper into home appliance and cosmetics markets once dominated by Japanese, South Korean, and European companies. Chinese firms already dominate EVs, consumer electronics, and home appliances; BYD is now the top car brand in most SEA markets and number-one in Singapore.

→ Downstream SEA impact: Intensifying price competition in AC, TV, and major appliance categories; Japanese brands face share erosion.
Source: KrASIA / Nikkei Asia · China Daily HK

Japanese Manufacturers Shift Production Out of China

More Japanese companies are moving production from China to Southeast Asia. Kyocera plans to shift part of its tool production to Vietnam to avoid US tariffs on Chinese exports. Japanese firms operating in China dropped from 14,394 in 2012 to 13,034 in 2023, reflecting a clear relocation trend.

→ Downstream SEA impact: Vietnam and Thailand gain new manufacturing capacity in precision components and tools.
Source: The China-Global South Project

US Tariff Exemptions Reshape SEA Manufacturing Calculus

New Section 301 tariffs apply on top of regular US import duties, replacing the temporary 10% surcharge that expired 24 July 2026. Country rates: Cambodia 10%, Indonesia 10%, Malaysia 10%, Philippines 12.5%, Singapore 12.5%, Thailand 12.5%, Vietnam 12.5%. Vietnam goods shipped via China to evade duties may face rates as high as 40%.

→ Downstream SEA impact: Vietnam remains cost-competitive but faces higher tariff risk; Malaysia and Thailand gain relative advantage for US-bound electronics.
Source: ASEAN Briefing · Instagram/Cashtag Business

China+1 Strategy: Vietnam Leads for Consumer Electronics

Vietnam is the top choice for consumer electronics assembly under China+1 strategies; Malaysia is preferred for semiconductor and technical electronics; Philippines for English-language assembly operations. China remains the deepest base for components, particularly high-density PCB assembly and specialty parts.

→ Downstream SEA impact: Continued FDI inflow into Vietnamese assembly hubs; Malaysia solidifies position in higher-tech segments.
Source: Cosmo Sourcing · SVI Global

Policy Focus

🇮🇩 Indonesia — Permendag 19/2026 E-Commerce Rules in Force

Minister of Trade Regulation No. 19 of 2026 took effect 8 June 2026, revoking Permendag No. 31/2023. Key provisions cover seller licensing, cross-border trade, fee transparency, complaint handling, domestic product prioritization, and AI governance. Foreign platforms and sellers must reassess PMSE operations against new requirements.

Status: In effect since 8 June 2026
⚠ Compliance Alert: Foreign e-commerce platforms selling electronics into Indonesia must verify seller licensing and domestic product prioritization compliance.

🇮🇩 Indonesia — TKDN Local Content for Electronics

TKDN (Tingkat Komponen Dalam Negeri) mandates a minimum 35% local content for 4G and 5G smartphones, tablets, and PCs under MoI Regulation No. 29/M-IND/PER/7/2017. TKDN certification is issued by the Ministry of Industry and is increasingly significant for electronics market access.

Status: Mandatory for 4G/5G devices

🇲🇾 Malaysia — MCMC Technical Codes Mandatory 5 November 2026

Malaysia's wireless certification requirements are changing, with new MCMC technical codes becoming mandatory on 5 November 2026. Manufacturers, laboratories, importers, and e-commerce sellers should confirm compliance plans before the transition affects certification timelines.

Status: Effective 5 November 2026
⚠ Compliance Alert: Wireless devices entering Malaysia after November 2026 must meet new MCMC technical codes; plan certification lead times accordingly.

🇸🇬 Singapore — Cybersecurity Labelling Scheme for IoT

By end of 2027, mandatory cybersecurity certification for household routers will be raised from CLS Level 1 to Level 2. The Cybersecurity Labelling Scheme rates smart consumer devices by cybersecurity provisions. CSA signed a Mutual Recognition Arrangement with the Connectivity Standards Alliance in March 2024 to reduce duplicated testing.

Status: Level 2 mandatory by end-2027

🇹🇭 Thailand — Duty-Free Parcel Exemption Ends

Effective 1 January 2026, Thailand ended the 1,500 THB duty-free exemption for parcels. All parcels from 1 THB now pay 7% VAT plus import duty. Most consumer goods face additional duties, tightening cross-border e-commerce import rules.

Status: In effect since 1 January 2026

Policy

TKDN Local Content Requirement: 35% for 4G/5G Devices

TKDN (Tingkat Komponen Dalam Negeri) mandates a minimum percentage of domestic components in smartphones, tablets, and certain electronics. For 4G and 5G devices, the minimum TKDN requirement is 35%, determined by the Directorate General of Resources and Equipment of Post and Informatics under MoI Regulation No. 29/M-IND/PER/7/2017.

TKDN certification is an official acknowledgment issued by the Ministry of Industry, certifying that a product meets the specified local content percentage. For electronic products, TKDN has become increasingly significant due to government efforts to encourage domestic manufacturing.

Compliance Alert

Smartphone brands importing into Indonesia must maintain 35% TKDN certification for 4G/5G devices; verify local content compliance before market entry.

2026 · Kickrate / ARMA Law · source
