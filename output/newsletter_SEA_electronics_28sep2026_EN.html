Policy Focus

Regulatory changes affecting market access and compliance

🇮🇩 Indonesia — Permendag 19/2026 overhauls e-commerce regulation

Indonesia's Ministry of Trade issued Regulation No. 19 of 2026 on the Implementation of E-Commerce Activities, replacing MOT Regulation No. 31 of 2023. The new regulation expands regulatory coverage, strengthens consumer protection, widens the categories of businesses subject to PMSE rules, tightens conditions for cross-border sellers, mandates algorithmic changes for domestic product promotion, introduces Indonesia's first AI governance rules in a commercial context, and makes platforms directly responsible for the compliance of the sellers they host. A five-tier sanctions framework backs all of it.

Separately, Indonesia's Trade Ministry has outlined new priorities for the digital commerce sector aimed at increasing local product participation and transparency. TKDN local content requirements for electronics carry concrete enforcement: operators (Telkomsel, Indosat, XL, Tri) block IMEIs of devices without complete certification, e-commerce platforms (Tokopedia, Shopee, Lazada, Blibli) increasingly require proof of TKDN before listing, and customs and tax authorities can demand reclamation of preferential duties where devices are found sold without proper TKDN.

Status: In force — MOT Reg. 19/2026 enacted 8 June 2026, replacing MOT Reg. 31/2023
⚠ Compliance Alert: Devices without complete TKDN certification cannot be activated on Indonesian networks and cannot be legally listed on major marketplaces.
21 Sep 2026 · OpenGov Asia / ABNR / AHP / Kickrate · Indonesia: Updated E-Commerce Rules Promote Local Products and Transparency

🇲🇾 Malaysia — MCMC new standards fully enforced from 5 November 2026

All subsequent related equipment submitted for certification must comply with two new standards. MCMC offers a six-month grace period, and the new regulations will be fully enforced starting 5 November 2026. The government encourages applicants to achieve compliance as soon as possible during the grace period. For consumer and industrial electronics, MCMC is involved as soon as a product carries an intentional emitter: Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, cellular, LPWA, remote control or active RFID.

Malaysia operates a two-organisation architecture for electronics certification — SIRIM and MCMC — comparable in logic to the CITC/SASO split in Saudi Arabia. The certificate holder must be a local business entity registered with the Companies Commission of Malaysia (SSM) and must possess suitable premises, such as a registered office or warehouse, to store and distribute the certified goods.

Status: Grace period active — full enforcement 5 November 2026
⚠ Compliance Alert: Radio-emitting devices submitted after the grace period must meet the two new standards; local SSM entity and premises required for certificate holding.

🇸🇬 Singapore — CSA to raise router cybersecurity labelling to CLS Level 2 by 2027

Permendag 19/2026 expands platform liability and adds Indonesia's first commercial AI rules

On 8 June 2026, the Minister of Trade enacted Regulation No. 19 of 2026 on Electronic Commerce, replacing Minister of Trade Regulation No. 31 of 2023. The new regulation significantly expands what e-commerce platforms — both domestic and foreign — must do, not just in terms of their own compliance, but in actively overseeing merchant activities, pricing practices, consumer-facing information and product traceability. It also introduces more serious consequences and a five-tier sanctions framework.

Regulation No. 19/2026 expands and updates the documents required to obtain a business licence for KP3A E-Commerce. Revised requirements include a legalised copy of the company's constitutional or incorporation documents prepared in accordance with the laws of the foreign e-commerce operator's country of origin, a deed of establishment prepared in accordance with those laws, and a self-declaration.

Other emerging markets include Thailand, Vietnam and the Philippines. Indonesia's +43.8% year-on-year appliance growth is the fastest in the region and broad-based across categories.

Leading companies in the Southeast Asia household appliances market include Samsung Electronics, LG Electronics and Panasonic, alongside Chinese challengers Midea, Haier, Hisense and GREE.

Market Signal

Indonesia's growth rate is roughly an order of magnitude above mature SEA markets — category planning should weight Indonesia as the primary volume engine for the next planning cycle.

Panasonic

Panasonic to shut Bangkok-area appliance factory, consolidate production to Vietnam

Panasonic will shut down a large appliance factory outside Bangkok as early as this fall and consolidate production to a larger facility in Vietnam for greater efficiency, according to Nikkei. The plant outside Hanoi would become the company's largest manufacturing hub for refrigerators and washing machines in Southeast Asia.

The move shifts Panasonic's large-appliance centre of gravity out of Thailand and into northern Vietnam, where the group will concentrate refrigerator and washing machine output for the region.

Sourcing Implication

Thai-based large appliance component and sub-assembly suppliers face volume loss; Vietnamese suppliers in the Hanoi corridor gain refrigerator and washer sub-tier demand.

Manufacturing

Midea Thailand RAC factory becomes home appliance industry's first WEF Lighthouse

Strategically located in Chonburi within Thailand's Eastern Economic Corridor, the Midea Thailand RAC factory spans 208,000 sqm with a total investment of approximately US$168 million. As the largest residential air conditioner production hub in Thailand and Southeast Asia, it plays a central role in shaping the region's smart manufacturing landscape. In 2026, the Midea Thailand RAC Factory's annual output is expected to reach 6 million units.

The facility has become the home appliance industry's first Lighthouse factory, setting a new standard in smart manufacturing. The designation is a global first for the appliance sector.

Strategic Read

A 6-million-unit RAC hub with Lighthouse automation in Chonburi sets the regional cost and quality benchmark that Thai and Vietnamese air-conditioner assembly must now match.

Haier

Haier expanding factories in Chonburi and Prachin Buri

Haier, TCL, Hisense, Midea and Changhong are rolling out aggressive expansion strategies. Haier is expanding factories in Chonburi and Prachin Buri in Thailand.

The expansion places Haier's Thai footprint in the same Eastern Economic Corridor and central-Thailand industrial belt as Midea's Chonburi RAC hub, concentrating Chinese appliance manufacturing capacity in a narrow geographic corridor.

Sourcing Implication

Two of China's largest appliance groups expanding in the same Thai industrial belt will tighten competition for local labour, land and component supply in Chonburi and Prachin Buri.

EMS

Cal-Comp remains largest EMS company in Thailand and Southeast Asia

Cal-Comp Electronics (Thailand) Public Company Limited was established on 4 December 1989 and listed on the Stock Exchange of Thailand in 2001 under the symbol CCET, and listed on the Taiwan Stock Exchange Corporation as Taiwan Depositary Receipts in 2003 under the symbol 9105. Cal-Comp is the largest Electronics Manufacturing Services company in Thailand and Southeast Asia, providing OEM and ODM services across a variety of product categories.

Thailand's EMS base also includes Fabrinet, headquartered in Bangkok, which provides advanced optical packaging and precision optical, electro-mechanical and electronic manufacturing services to OEMs of complex products including optical communication components, modules and subsystems, automotive components, medical devices, industrial lasers and sensors.

Sourcing Implication

Thailand's EMS depth — Cal-Comp for volume OEM/ODM and Fabrinet for high-mix optical and electro-mechanical — makes it the region's most capable non-Vietnam alternative for complex consumer electronics builds.
