Global Industry Shifts — SEA Impact

Chinese Appliance Brands Gain Ground in Southeast Asia

Chinese manufacturers are taking share from Japanese and Korean rivals across the region. In home laundry appliances, the Chinese share rose to 21.2% from 18.1%, with particularly strong gains in Thailand where it climbed to 23.2% from 15.3%. Haier and Midea are scaling up production in Thailand, positioning the country as both a key market and export hub.

→ Downstream SEA impact: Competitive pressure on Japanese/Korean brands intensifies; Thai manufacturing base becomes a regional export platform.
Source: Nikkei Asia / kr-asia

China+1 Strategy Solidifies Across SEA Manufacturing

Electronics supply chains in 2026 increasingly rely on a China+1 approach, diversifying manufacturing to Southeast Asia to mitigate geopolitical risk. Vietnam, Indonesia and Malaysia are the primary alternatives. Factory occupancy in key Vietnamese provinces is above 90%, with availability windows closing.

→ Downstream SEA impact: Capacity constraints emerging in Vietnam; Indonesia's large population and growing middle class make it increasingly attractive for both manufacturing and domestic consumption.
Source: Cosmo Sourcing / DocShipper / SVI Global

SEA Smartphone Market Shifts to Profitability

Regional smartphone shipments fell 9% YoY in Q1 2026 while ASPs rose 19% to a record US$349. Rising DRAM and NAND costs are forcing vendors to prioritise margins over volume. Samsung led with 21% share, followed by OPPO at 20% and Xiaomi at 17%.

→ Downstream SEA impact: Entry-level segment under pressure; vendors enforcing stricter pricing discipline and raising prices on existing models.
Source: Omdia / Android Headlines

Passive Components

MLCC Market Enters K-Shaped Recovery; Samsung Electro-Mechanics Raises Prices 30%

The MLCC market is bifurcated: high-capacitance, large-case grades for AI servers and automotive are in tight supply with 20-40% price increases, while commodity 0402/0603 parts remain in oversupply. Samsung Electro-Mechanics has announced a 30% price increase on certain MLCC products from 1 August 2026 and is accelerating plans for its first overseas MLCC plant in the Philippines.

📍 Philippines (new plant) · Status: Price increase effective 1 Aug 2026

Buyers should audit BOMs by case size and capacitance grade; large-case, high-capacitance parts face 20-40% cost increases.

Policy Focus — SEA Regulatory Updates

Indonesia: MOT Regulation 19/2026 Takes Effect

Indonesia's new e-commerce regulation expands coverage, introduces AI governance rules, tightens conditions for cross-border sellers, and mandates algorithmic changes for domestic product promotion. Platforms are now directly responsible for seller compliance. A five-tier sanctions framework backs the regulation.

Status: In effect since June 2026
Compliance Alert: All digital commerce businesses must comply; platforms face direct liability for hosted sellers.

Malaysia: MCMC Sets Implementation Dates for New Equipment Standards

Two new technical codes — MCMC MTSFB TC T022:2026 (Communications Equipment – Baseline Requirements) and an SRD specification — are effective 5 May 2026 with a six-month grace period. Full enforcement begins 5 November 2026.

Status: Grace period until 5 Nov 2026
Compliance Alert: All subsequent related equipment submitted for certification must comply with the new standards.

Singapore: Cybersecurity Labelling for Routers to Rise to CLS Level 2

CSA and IMDA will raise mandatory cybersecurity requirements for residential routers from CLS Level 1 to Level 2 by end-2027. Level 2 requires secure communications, secure storage of sensitive data, and robust authentication mechanisms.

Status: Mandatory by end-2027
Compliance Alert: Manufacturers and distributors of residential routers must prepare for heightened security benchmarks.

US Section 301 Tariffs: Vietnam and Thailand at 12.5%

Effective 24 July 2026, the US implemented new Section 301 tariffs on imports from 60 trading partners. Vietnam and Thailand face a 12.5% effective rate on electronics; Malaysia faces 10%. Smartphones, laptops, desktop computers, hard drives, keyboards and all automatic data processing machines are exempt across all 60 economies.

Status: In effect since 24 July 2026
Compliance Alert: Technology products exempt; non-tech electronics from Vietnam and Thailand face 12.5% duty.

Policy

Singapore to Raise Cybersecurity Labelling for Residential Routers to CLS Level 2

CSA and IMDA will raise mandatory cybersecurity requirements for residential routers from CLS Level 1 to Level 2 by end-2027. Level 2 requires secure communications, secure storage of sensitive data, and robust authentication mechanisms.

The CLS, launched in October 2020, was the first cybersecurity labelling scheme for consumer IoT devices. CSA has also concluded its public consultation on cybersecurity licensing framework updates, with mandatory certification requirements expected by end-2027.

Compliance Alert

Router manufacturers and distributors must prepare for heightened security benchmarks to maintain market access in Singapore.

Policy

MOT Regulation 19/2026 Overhauls E-Commerce Framework

Indonesia's Minister of Trade Regulation No. 19 of 2026 expands the scope of businesses covered, introduces new obligations for online platforms and merchants, and establishes the country's first comprehensive rules on AI use in e-commerce. Platforms are directly responsible for seller compliance.

The regulation replaces MOT Reg 31/2023 and includes a five-tier sanctions framework. Cross-border sellers face tighter conditions, and algorithms must be adjusted to promote domestic products.

Compliance Alert

All digital commerce businesses operating in Indonesia must review compliance obligations; platform liability is now explicit.

14 Jul 2026 · Dentons HPRP · source
