Global Industry Shifts

Samsung & LG vie for Hitachi's home appliance unit

Samsung Electronics and LG Electronics are actively competing to acquire Hitachi's Japanese home appliance division, a move that would consolidate Japanese and Korean appliance assets against Chinese competition.

→ Downstream SEA impact: Potential restructuring of Hitachi appliance distribution and service networks across Thailand, Malaysia and Indonesia.
Source: Digitimes · 02 Sep 2026

Chinese appliance brands gain ground across SEA

Chinese brands are taking share from Japanese rivals in TVs and air conditioners. Home laundry appliance share in SEA rose to 21.2% from 18.1%; in Thailand it climbed to 23.2% from 15.3%. Haier is expanding factories in Chonburi and Prachin Buri; Midea has invested over 2.2 billion baht in a new AC plant in Rayong.

→ Downstream SEA impact: Intensifying price competition in AC and laundry categories; Chinese brands positioning Thailand as export hub.
Source: Nikkei Asia · 2026

MLCC price pressure hits consumer electronics

Murata has raised prices 15–35% on certain high-capacitance MLCCs as AI server demand shifts capacity away from consumer segments. Samsung Electro-Mechanics and Taiyo Yuden have followed with price adjustments.

→ Downstream SEA impact: Higher BOM costs for SEA-based phone and appliance assemblers; supply tightness for standard MLCC grades.
Source: FTC Electronics · 2026

BOE iPhone OLED share declines; Samsung Display gains

BOE's shipments to Apple are expected to fall from 40M units last year to ~30M this year. Samsung Display is the only supplier expected to see increased small-to-medium OLED shipments in 2026, boosted by Apple's new foldable product.

→ Downstream SEA impact: Samsung Display's Vietnam OLED plant likely to absorb additional Apple orders; panel allocation shifts across SEA assembly sites.
Source: Sammy Fans / Omdia · 04 Sep 2026

Core Component Supply Chain Update

PCB/FPC — Zhen Ding trial production at Rayong plant

Zhen Ding Technology began trial production at its USD 2 billion Rayong, Thailand plant, adding 1.2 million m² of any-layer HDI capacity aimed at AI-server modules. Meiko Electronics completed its USD 200 million multilayer-board factory in Vietnam with 600,000 m² annual capacity.

📍 Thailand (Rayong) · Vietnam · Status: Trial production / Completed

New HDI and multilayer capacity in Thailand and Vietnam supports regional server and handset PCB demand.

Display — Samsung Display's USD 1.8B Vietnam OLED plant

Samsung Display is building a USD 1.8 billion OLED plant in Vietnam. Vietnam has become a major OLED manufacturing hub with USD 28 billion combined investment from LG and Samsung Display. Global display market projected at USD 150–167 billion in 2025, growing to ~USD 200 billion by 2030.

📍 Vietnam · Status: Under construction / Expansion

Expanding OLED capacity in Vietnam strengthens the country's position in smartphone and IT display supply chains.

Camera Modules — LG Innotek completes V3 plant in Vietnam

LG Innotek completed its new V3 camera module factory in Vietnam, doubling production capacity. The company plans a dual strategy: Gumi, Korea as the R&D "mother factory" for high-value modules, and Vietnam as the mass-production hub for standard products. The move aims to enhance cost competitiveness amid heated competition for smartphone orders.

📍 Vietnam (Hai Phong) · Status: Completed / Operations begun

Expanded Vietnam capacity positions LG Innotek to win additional smartphone camera module orders from Apple and other OEMs.

Battery — ATL/TDK silicon-anode cells; SEA battery show demand

TDK reported higher sales of small-capacity rechargeable batteries (ATL's main product line) in FY ended March 2026, driven by AI features in mobile devices. The Battery Show Asia 2026 reported 62.1% international buyers, with SEA buyer groups from Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand and Vietnam demonstrating robust demand.

📍 Regional · Status: Market expansion

AI-driven battery demand growth supports ATL's Vietnam and regional production footprint.

Passives — Samsung Electro-Mechanics targets MLCC leadership

Samsung Electro-Mechanics announced plans to double total sales by 2026 and achieve the leading spot in the global MLCC market for IT. The company is second only to Murata globally. AI-related demand is prioritising high-value, high-capacitance MLCCs for servers and data centres.

📍 Regional · Status: Capacity reallocation

Consumer-grade MLCC supply tightens as manufacturers shift capacity to AI server components.

Compressors/Motors — Nidec showcases appliance motor tech

Nidec Global Appliance showcased Embraco compressors and Nidec motor technologies at AWE 2026, including high-efficiency BLDC motors for washers and the new BLDC Gen 2.5 for dryers. Kulthorn compressors continue distribution across Laos, Thailand, Malaysia and Vietnam.

📍 Thailand · Status: Active

Policy Focus

🇮🇩 Indonesia — MOT Reg. 19/2026 e-commerce overhaul

Enacted 8 June 2026, replacing MOT Reg. 31/2023. Expands regulatory coverage, strengthens consumer protection, tightens conditions for cross-border sellers, mandates algorithmic changes for domestic product promotion, introduces Indonesia's first AI governance rules in a commercial context, and makes platforms directly responsible for seller compliance. Backed by a five-tier sanctions framework.

Status: In force · Compliance required
⚠ TKDN: Devices without complete certification are IMEI-blocked by operators (Telkomsel, Indosat, XL, Tri); e-commerce platforms increasingly require proof of TKDN before listing.

🇲🇾 Malaysia — MCMC consultation on roaming & eSIM

MCMC opened a public consultation on 1 September 2026 under the Communications and Multimedia Act 1998 on regulating international roaming-based connectivity services, including eSIM solutions. Covers roaming architecture, eSIM provisioning, cross-border consumer protection and conditions for non-resident providers.

Status: Public consultation open · Response required from MNOs

🇸🇬 Singapore — CSA raises router cybersecurity to Level 2

CSA and IMDA announced mandatory Cybersecurity Labelling Scheme (CLS) requirements for residential routers will be raised from Level 1 to Level 2 by 2027. Manufacturers must ensure secure communications, secure storage of sensitive data and robust authentication mechanisms. IMDA also published new August 2026 eUICC technical specifications for eSIM-enabled devices.

Status: Phased implementation to end-2027

🇻🇳 🇹🇭 🇵🇭 — US tariff frameworks signed

Vietnam's reciprocal rate held at 20% with potential 0% duty on some Annex III articles; Malaysia's rate maintained at 19% with similar Annex III extensions. Electronics from Vietnam face 12.5% effective US tariff rate; Thailand and Philippines also at 12.5%. Section 232 tariffs (25% steel / 10% aluminium) may apply to components.

Status: Framework agreements signed · Implementation pending

Policy

Indonesia enacts MOT Reg. 19/2026 — major e-commerce overhaul

Indonesia's Ministry of Trade enacted Regulation No. 19 of 2026 on 8 June 2026, replacing MOT Reg. 31/2023. The new regulation significantly expands what e-commerce platforms (both domestic and foreign) must do — not just their own compliance, but actively overseeing merchant activities, pricing practices, consumer-facing information and product traceability.

Key provisions include tightened conditions for cross-border sellers, mandated algorithmic changes for domestic product promotion, Indonesia's first AI governance rules in a commercial context, and direct platform responsibility for hosted seller compliance. A five-tier sanctions framework backs the regulation.

Compliance Alert

Foreign platforms and sellers must reassess PMSE operations against new requirements covering seller licensing, cross-border trade, fee transparency and AI governance.

2026 · Mondaq / ABNR · mondaq.com
Policy

TKDN local content rules reshape Indonesia's electronics market

Indonesia's TKDN (local content requirement) mandates a minimum of 40% locally produced components for electronics. Devices without complete certification cannot be activated on Indonesian networks — operators (Telkomsel, Indosat, XL, Tri) block IMEIs of uncertified devices.

E-commerce platforms (Tokopedia, Shopee, Lazada, Blibli) increasingly require proof of TKDN before listing. Customs and tax authorities can demand reclamation of preferential duties if devices are found sold without proper TKDN.

Compliance Alert

Importers and brands must secure TKDN certification before device launch to avoid IMEI blocking and platform delisting.

2026 · Kickrate · kickrate.com
