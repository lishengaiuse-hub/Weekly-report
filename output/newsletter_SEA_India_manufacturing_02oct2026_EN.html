Global Industry Shifts

Cross-border actions with named SEA/India impact

China Plus One: India must fix local supply chains and regulation to compete with Southeast Asia

Analysis published 26 September argues India needs stronger local supply chains, simpler regulations and reliable policy execution to compete for diverted investment as companies diversify. India is described as competitive in smartphone assembly, pharma, auto components and specialty chemicals, but constrained by higher wage floors and skilled-labour capacity limits for complex electronics assembly.

Component lead times remain the key gap: most components in India are still imported, adding 4–8 weeks to electronics BOM lead times versus hours in China.

→ Downstream SEA/India impact: reinforces the split sourcing pattern — India for assembly-heavy, component-light products; Vietnam, Thailand and Malaysia for component-dense work.

Factories and facilities in Southeast Asia and India only

Victory Giant Technology $260m Bac Ninh PCB plant set for October 2026 start

Victory Giant Technology's Vietnam PCB factory in VSIP Bac Ninh 2 Industrial Park covers 103,626 sq.m with investment capital of $260m. It will manufacture three million sq.m of high-precision multi-layer PCB and one million sq.m of high-density interconnect PCB annually, hiring approximately 2,300 workers on official operation in October 2026.

The company operates production bases in Guangdong, Hunan, Malaysia, Thailand and Vietnam with more than 20,000 employees. Its Vietnamese subsidiary was established in July 2024 with charter capital of VND720bn ($30m).

📍 VSIP Bac Ninh 2, Vietnam · Trial operations Jul–Sep 2026; official operation October 2026

Sourcing Implication: adds meaningful high-layer-count and HDI capacity inside Vietnam just as US Section 301 duties reshape landed-cost maths for boards shipped to US customers.

Murata begins production of 0201 three-terminal low-ESL MLCCs; Thai MLCC facility build continues

Murata has begun production of 0201 three-terminal low-ESL MLCCs for IC decoupling, announced 30 September 2026. Separately, the company is building a new Thai facility for MLCC capacitors, with construction of the Thailand MLCC plant having begun in July 2021 and completed in March 2023.

The launches land amid severe passive-component tightness: lead times on high-capacitance, low-ESL grades have stretched from 8–12 weeks in late 2024 to 26–40 weeks in 2026.

Sourcing Implication: three-terminal low-ESL parts are the decoupling workhorse for AI-server and high-density boards — lock in allocation early given 26–40 week lead times on comparable grades.

Policy Focus

Regulation and incentive changes affecting electronics manufacturing

🇮🇩 Indonesia — TKDN local-content reform under MOI Regulation No. 35 of 2025

Indonesia has relaxed TKDN requirements under Ministry of Industry Regulation No. 35 of 2025, with the Ministry of Industry working to revise the TKDN calculation methodology to simplify certification and promote investment in innovation. For 4G/5G smartphones, tablets and handheld devices the minimum requirement remains 35% TKDN; digital TV set-top boxes require a minimum 30%.

Calculation is based on component value, not physical quantity. Non-compliance can block SDPPI Certification for radio-frequency devices and prevent activation on Indonesian networks. The process from application to certificate issuance is now fully electronic through SIINas, with a 1-day completeness check and up to a 3-day correction window.

Status: Reform in force; methodology revision ongoing
Compliance Alert: certificate holders must report production and component composition periodically; re-audit may be required for significant changes.

🇲🇾 Malaysia — New Investment Incentive Framework (NIIF) applications open via Invest Malaysia

Malaysia's outcome-based, performance-driven NIIF launched in Q1 2026 for manufacturing and Q2 2026 for services, funded through an RM1bn strategic investment fund. Companies qualify for tiered tax preferences by meeting value-creation outcomes including high-skilled jobs, R&D, supply chain integration and digitalisation.

Applications for manufacturing incentives under the NIIF are accepted by MIDA via the Invest Malaysia portal from 1 March 2026. Budget 2026 also provides Accelerated Capital Allowance of 20% initial plus 40% annual allowance on plant, machinery, ICT and licensed software, with a capex window from 11 October 2025 to 31 December 2026.

Status: Applications open; capex window closes 31 December 2026

🇹🇭 Thailand — BOI and EEC incentives for smart electronics and automation

Thailand's BOI offers corporate income tax exemption for 8 years with no cap for companies developing automation and robotics solutions, and up to 15 years CIT exemption for factories in the Eastern Economic Corridor when combined with EEC-specific incentives. The combined BOI + EEC package in Chachoengsao, Chonburi and Rayong offers significantly enhanced benefits.

For smart electrical appliances and smart electronics the BOI lists a 40% measure, with applications required by the last working day of 2026 and "Made in Thailand" certification submitted before the CIT exemption expires and no later than the last working day of 2027.

Status: Applications open; deadline last working day of 2026

🇮🇳 India — Electronics Components Manufacturing Scheme (ECMS) outlay raised to ₹40,000 crore

ECMS provides turnover-linked incentives of 4–8% of incremental sales over a base year, capex-linked incentives of 25% of eligible capital expenditure, and hybrid structures combining both. The scheme runs six years from FY 2025-26 to FY 2031-32, with a one-year gestation period for turnover-linked incentives and a five-year period for capex incentives.

The Union Budget 2026–27 enhanced the ECMS outlay to ₹40,000 crore. Eligible manufacturers must meet investment, production and employment criteria. A 60-day fast-track approval mechanism has been introduced for strategic manufacturing sectors including electronics components, capital goods, semiconductors and solar manufacturing inputs.

Status: In force; outlay enhanced in Union Budget 2026–27

🇻🇳 Vietnam — Decree 342/2026/NĐ-CP tightens FDI trade licensing rules

Under Decree 342/2026/NĐ-CP, effective from 18 October, foreign-invested enterprises operating for at least one year must also have no overdue tax liabilities. The measure forms part of Vietnam's shift from volume-based investment attraction to a value-based, performance-oriented model.

Key policy directions include prioritising semiconductors, AI, digital technologies, biotechnology, clean energy and advanced manufacturing, and promoting stronger linkages between foreign-invested enterprises and domestic businesses.

Status: Effective 18 October 2026
Compliance Alert: FIE trade licence renewals will require a clean tax-liability position from 18 October.

🇸🇬 Singapore — CSA and IMDA to raise router cybersecurity labelling to CLS Level 2

The Cyber Security Agency of Singapore will work with IMDA to raise mandatory cybersecurity requirements for residential routers from Cybersecurity Labelling Scheme Level 1 to Level 2 by 2027. The change adds secure communications, secure storage of sensitive data and robust authentication mechanisms.

Separately, CSA concluded its public consultation on cybersecurity licensing framework updates, with key changes including mandatory certification requirements, extended five-year licence validity and streamlined notification processes, expected to come into force by end-2027.

Status: Announced; implementation targeted by 2027

BYD Electronics to start construction on $183.7m Phu Tho plant this month

BYD Electronics will start construction of a $183.7m plant in Vietnam's Phu Tho province this month, per a 28 September 2026 report. Separately, BYD plans a $144m expansion at its northern Vietnam plant covering USB chargers, in-car fast wireless chargers, robotic pool cleaners, lithium batteries, 4G/5G chips, GPS modules, gimbal stabilizers, ultra-light consumer drones and graphic processing boards.

The expansion is set to begin in October.

Sourcing Implication

BYD is converting Phu Tho from a handset-component site into a multi-category electronics campus — casing and repair capacity for Apple products is the notable new vector.

BreakingTata

Tata Electronics grows into strategically important arm with FY26 revenue of ₹1.31 trillion

Tata Electronics has grown rapidly from assembling iPhones to building semiconductor facilities, with revenue rising from ₹1.9 crore in FY22 to ₹1.31 trillion in FY26, reported 29 September 2026. The Tata Group is committing further capital to the business.

The company's workforce has expanded to 75,000, surpassing Taiwanese rival Foxconn's headcount in India and making it Apple's largest contract manufacturer by headcount. Growth has been driven primarily by its 500-acre Hosur facility in Tamil Nadu.

Strategic Read

Tata has become the anchor counterparty for Apple volume in India — supplier qualification strategies for India-assembled devices should be built around its Hosur and Narsapura footprint.

Dixon-Vivo JV Adivistar Electronics India to begin production in December quarter

Dixon Technologies has floated a subsidiary for its joint venture with Vivo Mobiles, named Adivistar Electronics India, holding a 51% stake with Vivo holding the remaining 49%. The partnership received government approval in July 2026 for smartphone production.

The JV is expected to add 20–22 million smartphone units to Dixon's annual production and generate around ₹30,000 crore in additional annual revenue once fully operational. Production is expected to begin in the December quarter of the current financial year, with around 11 million smartphones likely manufactured in FY27 before scaling up in FY28.

Daikin to expand M&A and investments across Southeast Asia and India

Daikin Industries is expanding its M&A and investment activity across Southeast Asia and India, reported 26 September 2026. LG

LG Electronics ₹5,000 crore Sri City plant to begin production by December 2026

LG Electronics is investing around $600m (₹5,000 crore) in a new manufacturing plant at Sri City, Andhra Pradesh, with production expected to begin by December 2026. The 247-acre facility will be LG's third manufacturing plant in India, after Greater Noida and Pune, and construction began in May last year.

The plant is expected to nearly double LG India's manufacturing capacity from the current 14.5 million units, with additional capacity added in phases over the next three financial years and project completion targeted for FY30. It is expected to create around 1,495 direct jobs. The first products will be air conditioners, followed by AC compressors, refrigerators and washing machines.

Sourcing Implication

AC compressor production at Sri City from December 2026 is the key milestone — it would localise a component India currently imports at scale.

Daikin India has surpassed the $1bn milestone and is targeting $2bn by 2026. The company intends to double its production capacity in India by establishing a new plant in the south and is considering another plant in the west.
